# Voice-first setup conversation: UX evidence and rules

Date: 2026-10-18. Scope: best-practice evidence for a voice-first, conversational onboarding on a mobile web app for plumbers and HVAC owner-operators. Competitor analysis is out of scope (see `competitive-onboarding-narrative-2026-10-18.md`). Sources are inline; where the only evidence is vendor marketing or secondhand teardown, it is labelled as such.

## 1. Voice UI principles from primary sources

**Google Conversation Design** (Assistant docs, still the most complete public guide):

- *Confirmations.* Implicit confirmation of parameters is the default: work what you heard into the next prompt ("Got it, Dave's Plumbing. What area do you cover?") so the user can catch an error without being asked to approve each slot. Explicit confirmation ("Did you say...?") is reserved for actions that are hard to reverse and for parameters where a misunderstanding is costly, with names and addresses given as the examples. Skip confirmation entirely for yes/no answers and global commands. [confirmations](https://developers.google.com/assistant/conversation-design/confirmations?hl=en)
- *Errors.* Treat No Match (heard something, could not use it) and No Input (silence, noise) separately. First retry is a "rapid reprompt": short apology plus a condensed version of the question, never the original prompt verbatim. Second retry adds detail or offers examples. Cap at three consecutive errors, then exit gracefully. [errors](https://developers.google.com/assistant/conversation-design/errors) / [Dialogflow reprompts](https://developers.google.com/assistant/df-asdk/reprompts)
- *Chips.* Every dialog turn has a spoken prompt, a display prompt and chips. "Be sure to add chips. At a minimum, these should include any options offered in the prompts so the user can quickly tap them to respond." Because the user may not be looking at the screen, any option offered as a chip must also be spoken. Wide-focus questions benefit most from chips; examples beat instructions. [suggestions](https://developers.google.com/assistant/conversation-design/suggestions?hl=en) / [scale your design](https://developers.google.com/assistant/conversation-design/scale-your-design)
- *Design voice first.* Write the screenless dialog first, then add the screen; the display prompt is a condensed, scannable version of the spoken one. [scale your design](https://developers.google.com/assistant/conversation-design/scale-your-design)
- *Cooperative principle.* Users treat the persona like a person and volunteer more than was asked ("Dave's Plumbing, we do drains and water heaters, mostly north side"). Design to accept over-answers, not reject them. [learn about conversation](https://developers.google.com/assistant/conversation-design/learn-about-conversation)

**Amazon Alexa design guide / Alexa Haus:**

- *Be brief.* The "one-breath test": if a prompt cannot be read aloud at conversational pace in one breath (Alexa Haus allows one or two), cut it. When a line fails, remove 2-5 words. Longer responses are harder to follow and remember. [be brief](https://developer.amazon.com/alexa/alexa-haus/design-principles/be-brief) / [one-breath test](https://developer.amazon.com/en-US/blogs/alexa/post/531ffdd7-acf3-43ca-9831-9c375b08afe0/things-every-alexa-skill-should-do-pass-the-one-breath-tes)
- *Specific questions, finite options.* Prompt with a specific question or a short list ("plane, train, car, or bus") rather than an open "how will you travel?". Do not read menus. [design the prompts](https://developer.amazon.com/en-AU/docs/alexa/interaction-model-design/design-the-prompts-for-your-skill.html)
- *Confirm selectively.* Confirm a slot with yes/no only where a mistake would be inconvenient; confirming every slot adds steps. Repeated errors on the same turn mean the user needs more help or has left. [handle the unexpected](https://developer.amazon.com/blogs/alexa/post/1a4f4efb-21e1-445b-af3e-1b740a12e6fd/things-every-alexa-skill-should-do-handle-the-unexpected-gracefully) / [Alexa Haus errors](https://developer.amazon.com/en-US/alexa/alexa-haus/patterns-and-components/patterns-errors)
- *Multimodal.* Visuals add to the voice experience; anything tappable must also be sayable, and "a skill should never require touch to progress." [10 tips for visual responses](https://developer.amazon.com/blogs/alexa/post/c99e2446-0f47-42e6-b59d-e9e901d688b4/10-tips-for-designing-alexa-skills-with-visual-responses)

**Apple HIG (Siri):** intents run resolve → confirm → handle. Disambiguation belongs in *resolve* ("which Amy?"), confirmation is for side-effecting actions, and response dialogue should state plainly what will happen. [HIG Siri](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/siri) / [developer forum on multi-step confirmation](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/832267)

**Nielsen Norman Group:**

- Voice assistants work "only for very limited, simple queries that have fairly simple, short answers"; participants reported speaking "like a robot" and accented speakers repeated themselves often. Recognition was the one acceptable dimension. Implication for setup: keep each turn to one short fact and never rely on free-form voice for anything long. [intelligent assistants study](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/intelligent-assistant-usability/) / [poor usability, high adoption](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/intelligent-assistants-poor-usability-high-adoption/)
- Response-time limits: under 0.1 s feels instant, under 1 s keeps flow, past ~10 s users leave unless shown progress. A voice turn that is "thinking" for more than a second needs a visible state. [response times](https://nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits)
- Deck-of-cards tutorials (70 users, 4 apps) made tasks feel *harder* and did not improve success. Onboarding is justified only when the app needs user information to tailor itself, which is exactly our case: keep it brief, highlight the unfamiliar, no tour. [mobile tutorials](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tutorials/)
- NN/g chatbot study: bots work best as "linear flows with a limited number of branches" and fail when users step off the path. [summary](https://blog.experientia.com/?p=20115)

**Interruption (barge-in).** Platform docs are thin; vendor engineering guidance converges on: keep ASR running during TTS, stop playback locally in under ~200 ms, use echo cancellation so the agent does not interrupt itself, and make confirmations and legal disclosures harder to barge through than ordinary prompts. A false barge-in is a worse failure than a slow one. [Deepgram](https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/flux-tts/interrupt-handling) / [NVIDIA](https://perspectives.nvidia.com/nemotron-speech/task/faq/how-do-i-implement-barge-in-detection-so-a-user-can-interrupt-a-voice-agent-whil/) / [Cognigy](https://www.cognigy.com/glossary/what-is-barge-in)

## 2. Consumer onboarding patterns with evidence

- **Duolingo (primary evidence).** Gina Gotthilf, First Round interview: moving sign-up back a few steps so users complete a lesson first raised DAU about 20%; later tests tuned *when* to ask and the copy of the "discard my progress" button. One decision per screen, progress bar, value before account. [First Round](https://review.firstround.com/the-tenets-of-a-b-testing-from-duolingos-master-growth-hacker) / [Taplytics](https://taplytics.com/blog/duolingo-ab-test-onboarding)
- **Shopify.** Sign-up opens with one single-select question ("Which best describes you?" → just starting / already selling), then a few use-case questions whose answers shape the setup checklist. Secondhand teardown. [Candu](https://www.candu.ai/blog/shopify-onboarding-flow). The Winter '26 AI store builder starts from a one-sentence description of the brand. [Techtic](https://www.techtic.com/blog/shopify-winter-2026-editions-ai-commerce/)
- **Square.** Asks business name as it should appear on receipts, phone, address, estimated revenue, then identity; its tips page tells sellers what to gather first. [Square help](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8329-onboard-your-sole-proprietorship-to-square) / [tips](https://squareup.com/ie/en/townsquare/onboarding-tips-with-square)
- **Monzo / Revolut Business.** ~10 minutes if documents are ready; eligibility is screened early because failing late is the main complaint. [Statrys](https://statrys.com/reviews/open-monzo-business-account) / [Monzo eligibility](https://monzo.com/business-banking/eligibility)
- **Headspace / Calm.** Three to five goal questions before pricing, home screen adapts to answers; critics note personalization must *visibly* change what comes next or it feels like a tax. Teardowns only. [Tear Them Down](https://tearthemdown.substack.com/p/headspace) / [Calm](https://screensdesign.com/articles/calm-onboarding-design/)
- **Cash App.** One field per screen (phone, then $cashtag). Design commentary, not a teardown. [gluestack](https://market.gluestack.io/blog/cash-app-design)
- **ChatGPT voice / Pi.** ChatGPT's late-2025 redesign moved voice into the chat thread with a live transcript by default (the separate orb screen is now an opt-in toggle), after repeated user requests to see text while talking. Pi uses a wave for listening and a distinct abstract animation for thinking. [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/chatgpt-voice-mode-in-chat-3619633) / [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/?p=1029981) / [OpenAI community](https://community.openai.com/t/enhancement-suggestion-live-text-display-in-voice-mode-for-better-ux/1122982) / [Pi review](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/pi-personal-ai-assistant)

**What the best do in the first 60 seconds:** show value or a result before asking for anything; ask exactly one easy question that visibly changes what follows; show progress; defer identity and payment; screen hard eligibility blockers early rather than late.

## 3. Configuring a business or account by speaking

Direct precedents are thin. Shopify Sidekick (chat, with voice added in Winter '26) takes a described task, proposes changes and asks for approval before applying them, the closest analogue to our proposal/approval gate. [Ringly](https://www.ringly.io/blog/ai-sidekick-shopify) / [Techtic](https://www.techtic.com/blog/shopify-winter-2026-editions-ai-commerce/). Intuit Assist "streamlines setup" but hands new users to a human expert rather than a spoken flow. [BusinessWire 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241120869309/en). Siri Shortcuts' *Ask Each Time* pauses an action to collect a missing parameter one at a time, a useful mental model for slot-filling. [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-in/guide/shortcuts/use-the-ask-each-time-variable-apd8b28e2166/ios). Home-service AI receptionists (OnCrew, CloudTalk, DialCatch) set up from a typed description or claim "zero setup"; none onboards by voice. [SourceForge](https://sourceforge.net/software/product/Callbook/) / [CloudTalk](https://www.cloudtalk.io/ai-voice-agent-for-plumbers/)

The academic evidence is a warning: **FormBharo** (2026 preprint) found form-completion accuracy fell 7-14 points (one model, 41) when input was real speech rather than clean transcripts, because per-field errors compound across turns; a normalisation layer and per-turn correction recovered much of it. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.06027). Patnaik et al. (ICTD 2009) found voice-to-operator entry had a 0.45% error rate against 4.2% for typed forms, but that was a human listener. [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=341261)

## 4. Form vs conversation: completion evidence

- **GOV.UK "one thing per page"**: each page holds one question or decision; low-confidence users find it easier, it works on mobile, errors and branching are simpler; Register to Vote kept it even on desktop because users said it "felt simple and easy" despite more clicks. [Design notes](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2015/07/03/one-thing-per-page/) / [Question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages) / [Form structure](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/form-structure) / [Nava case study](https://www.navapbc.com/uploads/Structuring%20a%20complex%20eligibility%20form%20for%20HealthCare.gov.pdf)
- **Typeform**: average completion 57% (56% mobile) on its own data; vendor figure. [Typeform help](https://help.typeform.com/hc/en-us/articles/360029615911). **Landbot** claims 2-3x; unverified. [Softabase](https://softabase.com/en/software/ai-chatbots/landbot). Multi-step vs single-page: Formstack's 650k-submission analysis reports +25.4%. Third-party commentary agrees the gain is for *longer* forms; under five fields a conversational layout can lose. [tinycommand](https://tinycommand.com/blogs/conversational-forms-vs-traditional-forms-which-is-better-for-your-business)
- **Takeaway:** direction is consistent, magnitude is vendor-inflated. Our setup has 8-12 facts, which is in the range where one-question-per-turn helps.

## 5. Tradespeople: environment and accessibility

- **Noise.** ASR degrades sharply with SNR: one 2022 study measured 5.7% word accuracy at -10 dB vs 93.8% at +20 dB in non-stationary noise; a 2024 HoloLens study (22 participants) found industrial noise hurt dictation WER badly and concluded ASR is "not yet a capable interaction paradigm in an industrial context". Short commands and constrained answers survive noise far better than dictation. [IEEE Access](https://doaj.org/article/b43e491a9c30469c82ce0d5d0101b1e8) / [MTI 2024](https://mdpi.com/2414-4088/8/2/8) / [Gothenburg thesis](https://gupea.ub.gu.se/items/b267bc8a-5361-4d54-9a79-4357d9d1300b)
- **Gloves.** Thicker gloves lower capacitance; the practical fix is larger targets or physical buttons. A construction wearable abandoned touch for pushbuttons after field tests requiring glove use and sunlight legibility. [Minatec](https://www.minatec.org/en/a-connected-armband-for-construction-workers) / [US 9817517](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9817517)
- **Sun.** Sub-500-nit phones wash out; high contrast and large type matter more than colour. [CrownTV](https://www.crowntv-us.com/blog/what-are-display-nits)
- **One hand.** Hoober's 1,333 street observations: 49% one-handed, 36% cradled; the centre of the screen is touched most accurately. Put the primary action in the lower centre, not a corner. [UXmatters](https://uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2013/02/how-do-users-really-hold-mobile-devices.php)
- **Targets.** Apple 44 pt, Material 48 dp, WCAG 2.5.8 minimum 24 px; use 44+ (our min-h-11 rule). [disabilityworld](https://www.disabilityworld.org/toolkit/glossary/touch-target-size/)
- **Transcript.** WCAG 1.2.9 requires a real-time text alternative for live audio; a transcript after the fact does not count. For us: show the live transcript and chips during every turn. [WCAG 1.2.9](https://wcag.dock.codes/rewrite/en/wcag129)

## Ten rules for our setup conversation

1. **One fact per turn.** One question, one short answer, one chip row. Never two questions in a prompt. (GOV.UK, Alexa, NN/g chatbots)
2. **Confirm implicitly by default; explicitly only for names, addresses, phone numbers and money.** Repeat the value in the next question; ask "did you say...?" only where a miss costs the business. (Google)
3. **Finite answer set? Say the options and show them as chips.** Everything spoken is tappable, everything tappable is spoken. (Google, Alexa)
4. **Low confidence gets one clarifying question, not a guess.** Rapid reprompt first, example-rich reprompt second, offer typing on the third. (Google errors, resolver rule in CLAUDE.md)
5. **Pass the one-breath test.** Every prompt under ~20 words. Cut 2-5 words when it fails. (Alexa)
6. **Always show listening / thinking / speaking, and the live transcript.** Anything over 1 s needs a visible state. (NN/g, WCAG 1.2.9, ChatGPT redesign)
7. **Accept over-answers.** If they volunteer three facts, capture all three and skip those questions. (Google cooperative principle)
8. **The user can interrupt, and can always type or tap instead.** Barge-in under 200 ms; never require voice to progress. (Alexa multimodal, barge-in guidance)
9. **Value before identity.** First 60 s: name the business, hear the agent answer a sample call, then ask for the account. Screen eligibility blockers early. (Duolingo, Monzo)
10. **Design for the truck.** 44 px targets in the lower centre, high contrast, short constrained answers that survive noise, and a visible "finish later" that saves progress. (Hoober, noise studies, GOV.UK)
