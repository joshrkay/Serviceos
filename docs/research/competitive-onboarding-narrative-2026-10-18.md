# Competitor onboarding + narrative brief — 2026-10-18

**Purpose.** Input to the onboarding/value-narrative redesign: a brand-new owner-operator who does not understand billing or back-office work should get set up by talking, and the product should feel like a service, not software. For each competitor: how the homepage *frames* the promise, how it *onboards*, what it *charges up front*, how it *looks*, and whether anyone configures by conversation.

## 0. What the internal research already concludes (not repeated here)

1. **Our structural lead is the trust architecture, not feature count** — typed proposals, approval, 5-second undo, audit, catalog-grounded prices, clarification instead of guessing; no competitor shows evidence of it (`competitive-2026-09-06/README.md` §0).
2. **Jobber is the head-to-head and its AI now writes to the system** (Jobber AI Voice/Chat, Receptionist $29/mo for 30 conversations); the wedge is trust + closed-loop receptionist + flat $99 vs a realistic $377–527/mo Jobber stack (`competitive-review-rivet-vs-jobber-2026-07-02.md`).
3. **Avoca is enterprise, demo-gated, implementation-team onboarded (4–12 weeks reported), ~$1–3.5k/mo**; its moat is knowledge base + correction flywheel + human backstop + ServiceTitan depth; SMB is explicitly underserved (`avoca-competitive-analysis.md` §7–8).
4. **ServiceTitan is the graduation risk, not a competitor at this ICP** — $245–500/tech/mo, $5–50k implementation, months to onboard; Workiz hides prices; HCP/Workiz suffer add-on creep (`appendix-d`, `-f`, `-g`).
5. **Our onboarding-adjacent Tier-1 gaps:** no import from Jobber/HCP, AI queues instead of books by default, QuickBooks one-way; the ICP buys on never-miss-a-call, get-paid-with-less-typing, look-professional, keep-the-accountant-happy (`competitive-gap-analysis.md`).

## 3. Jobs-to-be-done the category markets against

1. **When the phone rings while I'm under a sink, I want it answered and the job booked, so I don't lose the work to whoever picks up.** Owned by everyone with an AI receptionist (Sameday, Smith.ai, Jobber, HCP, Workiz, Avoca, Goodcall, Rosie, Broccoli). Crowded.
2. **When I finish a job, I want to get paid before I leave the driveway, so I'm not chasing invoices at night.** Incumbents (Jobber "quote, schedule, invoice, get paid", HCP, Service Fusion); among AI entrants only WorkHero/Carly. Under-owned.
3. **When a lead comes in online, I want it followed up in seconds, so it doesn't go cold.** Avoca Nurture, Probook, Workiz Genius Marketing, Broccoli Hailey. Mid-market owned.
4. **When I'm growing, I want to see what's working, so I can run a stronger business.** Jobber hero, FieldPulse, Workiz "growth engine", ServiceTitan Titan Intelligence, HCP Coach AI.
5. **When I switch tools, I want my customers and prices brought over, so I don't start from zero.** HCP (free basic / MAX team), ServiceTitan (analysts), Jobber (CSV/QBO). Nobody offers self-serve import *from a named competitor*; we offer none.
6. **When I hand my phone to an AI, I want to hear it first and keep it on a leash, so it never embarrasses me.** Goodcall (practice calls, publish gate), Jobber (Play preview, "asks before taking action"), HCP (Test CSR AI), Avoca (human backstop). Present in help docs, never the headline.
7. **When I'm a one-truck shop, I want a price I can understand today, so I'm not surprised later.** Rosie ($49 in the hero), Goodcall, Smith.ai, Jobber. Opaque: ServiceTitan, Avoca, Workiz, FieldPulse, Probook, Broccoli.
8. **When I don't know how to run an office, I want someone to set it up with me, so I never have to learn software.** Delivered only by humans at a price (ServiceTitan, FieldPulse, Service Fusion, HCP advisor, Abby). Nobody delivers it through the product itself.

## 4. Gaps nobody owns — claimable by a voice-first back office

- **"Set up by talking" for the whole business, not just the phone agent.** Goodcall proves owners accept conversational setup plus a publish gate — for a receptionist. No one lets an owner *say* "I charge $89 to come out, $150 an hour after, I take cards, Mon–Sat 7 to 5, I don't do gas" and get a price book, tax, payment rails, hours and booking rules drafted for approval.
- **"You don't need to know what an invoice is."** Every flow assumes the owner understands estimate vs invoice vs deposit vs progress billing; ServiceTitan literally asks you to "prepare your pricebook, data, and accounting" before kickoff. Translating back-office vocabulary into trade vocabulary is unclaimed.
- **Day-one proof by phone.** Jobber's Play preview is the closest (greeting only). A product that *calls the owner*, role-plays a customer, books a test job and texts the resulting proposal is unowned — and it is the natural moment to teach the approval gate.
- **Approval as the narrative.** Only Goodcall ("nothing changes until you publish") and Jobber ("asks before taking action") say it, and only in help docs. "It asks before it acts" as a hero line is open.
- **Service feel at SMB price.** Human onboarding is gated behind MAX/Pro/enterprise; self-serve is screenshots and checklists. Named agents (Amy, Hailey, Jessica, Gabi) and Smith.ai's "AI workforce… fully staffed" are the closest to staff-not-software; none pairs it with a flat SMB price.
- **Back office beyond the phone.** The 2025–26 wave (Avoca, Probook, Broccoli, Sameday, Rosie, Goodcall) is intake. Only WorkHero (HVAC-only, seed) says "back office". Calls *and* money handled by one thing has no name in the market.
- **Import from a photo.** No vendor turns a paper price list, invoice pad or competitor export into a working account; we already run vision in the gateway.

## 5. Patterns to steal, anti-patterns to avoid

**Steal**
1. **Goodcall — describe → draft → practice the hard cases → publish.** Gabi drafts from a description or website, runs practice calls on "a caller who wants a price or an emergency at midnight", shows results, and nothing goes live until approved; edits are plain English, typed or spoken. This is our proposal gate expressed as onboarding.
2. **Jobber — four human questions, then tailor; and the product calls you.** Industry, team size, years in business, goals → a checklist with time estimates and industry defaults. "Play preview" rings the owner's phone so they hear the receptionist before customers do.
3. **Housecall Pro — ship the trade's defaults and meet the owner on SMS.** Default price book "with homeowner-friendly descriptions" per trade, packages "preconfigured… with minimal customization", onboarding delivered as a text an hour after purchase, and a one-tap "Test CSR AI".
4. **Rosie / Sameday / Broccoli — one concrete promise in the hero.** "Starting at $49/month", "start taking calls in just 15 minutes", "2 days onboarding time" as a headline metric; named agents make it feel like hiring staff. (Sameday then breaks its promise with "schedule a demo" — don't.)
5. **Avoca / FieldPulse — show readiness the way a service would.** Avoca's per-capability Not Started / In Progress / Live tracker and go-live call; FieldPulse's sessions with homework between them. Spoken ("your line is live; estimates are ready; payments need your bank") this becomes a progress narrative, not a settings page.

**Avoid**
1. **Demo-gated, price-hidden entry** (ServiceTitan, Avoca, Workiz, FieldPulse, Service Fusion, Probook, Broccoli). A solo owner evaluates at 9 pm on a phone; "Book a demo" and a 12–16-week implementation disqualify, and the third-party pricing posts exist because vendors hide it.
2. **Add-on sprawl and gated help** (HCP migration team MAX-only and unpriced CSR AI; Jobber's 30-conversation cap; Workiz $200–300/mo modules; Goodcall/Avoca per-caller and per-minute bills producing a 2.5 Trustpilot and "busy-season spikes"). Our flat price is a narrative asset; the hero should say so.
3. **Software-first wizards that make the owner do office work to get software that promises to do office work** (Workiz: logo, service areas, roles before value; Jobber "First Steps" settings checklist; ServiceTitan "prepare your pricebook… before the Kick-Off Call"; Bland "no visual builder"). The first five minutes should produce a booked test job or a sent estimate, not a configured account.
